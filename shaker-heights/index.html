Keeping Your Shaker Heights Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year in hard-water areas.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a standard tank usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like discolored hot water, popping noises, or a damp spot around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet that same buildup, neglected through a few more years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and shorten the life of the tank, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number on your phone now so help is one call away as soon as something sounds or looks wrong. A quick call saves time, money, and cold showers.
