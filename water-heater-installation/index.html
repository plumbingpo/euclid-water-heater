Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • A heater squeezed into a closet or attic where a leak would do real damage
  • Plans to change fuel types or go all-electric
  • An older install without an expansion tank, pan, or straps where required
  • A large soaking tub that a standard tank cannot fill
  • Long waits for hot water at faucets far from the heater
  • Morning showers that run cold for the last person
  • Remodel plans that add fixtures or a second bathroom
  • New construction or a major renovation that needs a heater from scratch

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Installing a new water heater means doing several trades' work at once. The gas piping must be sized for the heater plus every other appliance on the line, then pressure-tested until it is leak-free. Venting has to follow the manufacturer's rules for diameter, length, pitch, and clearance to combustibles, or carbon monoxide can spill back into the house. Electric and heat pump units need a circuit, breaker, and wire gauge matched to their load, and whole-house electric tankless units can draw more current than many panels have to spare. On the water side, there is more: shutoffs, dielectric transitions, a correctly rated relief valve with a full-size discharge line, and code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps that differ from place to place.

Professional installation gets every piece right the first time. Our experienced technicians size the heater, calculate the gas line, lay out the vent route, confirm electrical capacity, and add the code items your location calls for. Leaks, draft, and carbon monoxide are tested, and the heater is left at a safe temperature with the paperwork handled. That is the difference between a heater that simply works for years and one that fails inspection or backdrafts on the first cold morning. And if a simpler option would serve you just as well, we tell you that too, without pressure.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Plan an annual descaling if you installed a tankless unit
  • Glance at the pan and discharge pipe every month or two; water there means it is time to call
  • Flush a tank every six to twelve months to keep sediment from settling
  • Keep a clear path to the heater so it can be serviced and inspected easily
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years to protect the tank
  • Set the thermostat near 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Store paint, gasoline, and solvents far from any gas water heater
