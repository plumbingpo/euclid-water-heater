How South Euclid Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year in hard-water areas.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a standard tank usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or a damp spot under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and is far less stressful than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A tank that's just begun to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet that same buildup, ignored through a few more years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten the life of the tank, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number on your phone today so help is one call away the moment something sounds or looks wrong. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.
